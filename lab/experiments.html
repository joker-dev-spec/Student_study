🔬 Experiment 1: Test for Starch in a Leaf

Aim: To show that photosynthesis produces starch in a green leaf.

Apparatus

  • A green leaf from a plant exposed to sunlight
  • Bunsen burner, boiling tube, water bath
  • Methylated spirit (alcohol), iodine solution
  • White tile, forceps

Procedure

1

Boil the leaf

Dip the leaf in boiling water for about 1 minute to kill the cells and break down cell walls.

2

Decolourise

Turn off the flame. Place the leaf in a boiling tube of alcohol and stand it in a hot water bath until the leaf becomes pale/white. The alcohol removes chlorophyll.

3

Wash

Dip the decolourised leaf in hot water to soften it and remove excess alcohol.

4

Add iodine

Spread the leaf on a white tile and add a few drops of iodine solution.

Expected Result

The leaf turns blue-black, showing that starch is present. This proves the leaf carried out photosynthesis.

Safety Tip Never heat alcohol directly over a flame — it is highly flammable. Use a water bath instead.

🧪 Experiment 2: Test for Water using Copper(II) Sulphate

Aim: To detect the presence of water in a substance.

Apparatus

  • Anhydrous copper(II) sulphate (white powder)
  • Watch glass, dropper, water sample

Procedure

1

Place the powder

Put a small amount of anhydrous copper(II) sulphate on a dry watch glass (it is white).

2

Add the sample

Add a few drops of the liquid being tested.

Expected Result

The white powder turns blue, confirming the presence of water (hydration of CuSO₄).

ReactionCuSO₄ (white) + 5H₂O → CuSO₄·5H₂O (blue)

⚛️ Experiment 3: Verification of Hooke's Law

Aim: To verify that the extension of a spring is proportional to the applied force.

Apparatus

  • A spring suspended from a clamp and stand
  • Mass hangers and slotted masses (50 g each)
  • Metre rule, pointer

Procedure

1

Record original length

Note the pointer position with no load (the original length).

2

Add masses

Add 50 g masses one at a time and record the new pointer position for each. The extension = new length − original length.

3

Repeat

Continue adding masses until you have at least 6 readings.

4

Plot graph

Plot a graph of extension (y-axis) against force/weight (x-axis).

Expected Result

The graph is a straight line through the origin, verifying Hooke's Law (F = ke). The slope of the graph gives the reciprocal of the spring constant (1/k).

Diagram DescriptionA spring hanging from a clamp. A pointer attached to the bottom of the spring moves down a metre rule as slotted masses are added to a hanger. Increasing the load increases the extension.
Exam TipBeyond the elastic limit, the graph curves and the spring deforms permanently — Hooke's Law no longer applies.

📐 Experiment 4: Verification of the Principle of Moments

Aim: To verify that clockwise moments equal anticlockwise moments for a body in equilibrium.

Apparatus

  • A uniform metre rule, knife-edge (pivot)
  • Known masses, thread loops

Procedure

1

Balance the rule

Balance the metre rule on the knife-edge to find its centre of gravity (usually near 50 cm).

2

Hang masses

Hang a known mass on the left and balance it with known masses on the right, then adjust positions until balanced.

3

Record

Note each mass and its distance from the pivot.

4

Verify

Confirm that (clockwise force × distance) = (anticlockwise force × distance).

Expected Result

The two moment totals are equal, confirming the principle of moments.
