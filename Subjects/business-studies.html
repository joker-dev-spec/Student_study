🏢 Introduction to Business

Definition

Business is any lawful activity concerned with the production, buying and selling of goods and services in order to make a profit and satisfy human wants.

Key Concepts

  • Goods are tangible (e.g. bread, shoes); services are intangible (e.g. teaching, banking).
  • Aim of business: to make profit, provide employment and satisfy wants.
  • Human wants are unlimited, but resources are limited, so we must make choices (scale of preference).
Profit Profit = Total Revenue − Total Cost
Diagram Description A flow showing: Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer, with goods moving forward and money moving backward.
Worked Example A trader sells goods for ₦50,000 and spent ₦35,000 to buy them. Find the profit.
Profit = ₦50,000 − ₦35,000 = ₦15,000
Exam Tip Be able to distinguish goods from services and give examples of each. Remember the chain of distribution.

🗂️ Office Practice and Equipment

Definition

An office is a place where paperwork and information are processed to support a business. Office practice is the organisation of office work and the use of office equipment.

Key Concepts

  • Office functions: receiving and sending information, keeping records, computing and protecting assets.
  • Office equipment: filing cabinet, computer, printer, photocopier, telephone, stapler, franking machine.
  • Filing methods: alphabetical, numerical, chronological and subject filing.
Good Filing System Simple · Accessible · Safe · Economical · Flexible
Diagram Description An office layout with a reception, work desks fitted with computers, a filing cabinet, a printer and a store room.
Worked Example Which filing method arranges files according to the letters of the alphabet?
The alphabetical filing method (arranged from A to Z).
Exam Tip Match each piece of office equipment to its function — questions often ask "which equipment is used for…?".

📒 Book-keeping (Double Entry)

Definition

Book-keeping is the recording of business transactions in the books of account. The double-entry principle states that every transaction has two effects: a debit and a credit of equal amount.

Key Concepts

  • Assets: things owned by the business (cash, stock, buildings).
  • Liabilities: debts owed by the business.
  • Capital: the owner's investment in the business.
Accounting Equation Assets = Capital + Liabilities
Diagram Description A simple "T" account with a debit (Dr) side on the left and a credit (Cr) side on the right, used to record two equal entries per transaction.
Worked Example The owner puts ₦200,000 into the business. Show the double entry.
Debit: Cash ₦200,000 ; Credit: Capital ₦200,000
Exam Tip Use the accounting equation to find any missing figure. Capital = Assets − Liabilities.

🛒 Principles of Commerce (Trade)

Definition

Commerce is the study of trade and aids to trade. Trade is the buying and selling of goods and services. It is divided into home trade and foreign trade.

Key Concepts

  • Home (internal) trade: wholesale and retail trade within a country.
  • Foreign trade: import, export and entrepot trade.
  • Aids to trade: banking, insurance, transport, warehousing, advertising and communication.
Division of Trade Trade = Home Trade (Wholesale + Retail) + Foreign Trade (Import + Export)
Diagram Description A tree diagram: root "Trade" branching into "Home Trade" and "Foreign Trade", with the aids to trade listed around the branches.
Worked Example Name two aids to trade and state how each helps commerce.
1) Banking — provides a safe place to keep money and make payments. 2) Transport — moves goods from producers to consumers.
Exam Tip Learn the meaning of import, export and entrepot trade, and be able to list aids to trade with their functions.

🏛️ Business Organisations

Definition

A business organisation is the structure through which business activity is carried out. Common types are the sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability company and co-operative society.

Key Concepts

  • Sole proprietorship: owned by one person; easy to set up but unlimited liability.
  • Partnership: 2–20 persons; shared capital, skills and profits.
  • Limited liability company: separate legal entity; shareholders' liability is limited.
  • Co-operative society: owned by members for mutual benefit.
Public vs Private Public company = PLC (shares sold to the public) · Private = Ltd (few shareholders)
Diagram Description A comparison table of the four business organisations across ownership, liability, capital and continuity.
Worked Example Which business organisation is owned and controlled by one person?
A sole proprietorship (sole trader).
Exam Tip Know the advantages and disadvantages of the sole proprietorship and partnership — they are the most frequently examined.
