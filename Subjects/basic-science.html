🧱 Matter

Definition

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. Everything around us — air, water, rocks, plants and animals — is made of matter. Matter exists in three states: solid, liquid and gas.

Key Concepts

  • Solid: fixed shape and volume; particles are tightly packed and vibrate in place.
  • Liquid: fixed volume but takes the shape of its container; particles are close but can flow.
  • Gas: no fixed shape or volume; particles move freely and fill any container.
  • Change of state: melting (solid→liquid), freezing (liquid→solid), evaporation (liquid→gas), condensation (gas→liquid).
Particle Arrangement Solid (packed, ordered) → Liquid (close, random) → Gas (far apart, fast)
Diagram Description Three containers side by side. In the first, particles are arranged in neat rows (solid). In the second, particles are jumbled together at the bottom (liquid). In the third, particles are spread far apart throughout the container (gas).
Worked Example Ice at 0 °C is heated. Describe the changes of state it undergoes.
Ice (solid) melts to water (liquid) at 0 °C, then water boils to steam (gas) at 100 °C.
Exam Tip Learn the particle diagrams for the three states — questions often ask you to compare how particles are arranged and how they move.

🌱 Living and Non-Living Things

Definition

Living things carry out the seven life processes, remembered by MRS GREN: Movement, Respiration, Sensitivity, Growth, Reproduction, Excretion and Nutrition. Non-living things do not perform all these processes.

Key Concepts

  • Nutrition: plants make food by photosynthesis; animals eat food.
  • Respiration: releasing energy from food inside cells.
  • Excretion: removal of waste products made by the body.
  • Reproduction: producing young ones to continue the species.
MRS GREN Movement · Respiration · Sensitivity · Growth · Reproduction · Excretion · Nutrition
Diagram Description A table comparing a mango tree (living) and a stone (non-living) across the seven life processes — the tree ticks every process, the stone ticks none.
Worked Example A car moves and uses fuel. Is it a living thing?
No. Although a car moves and uses fuel, it cannot grow, reproduce, excrete or respond to stimuli by itself. It is non-living.
Exam Tip Movement alone does not make something living — a river flows and a car moves. Apply all seven characteristics before deciding.

⚡ Energy

Definition

Energy is the ability to do work. It is measured in joules (J). Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another (the law of conservation of energy).

Key Concepts

  • Forms: light, heat, sound, electrical, chemical, mechanical (kinetic + potential), nuclear.
  • Sources: the sun, fossil fuels, wind, water (hydro), and the food we eat.
  • Energy transfer: a torch changes chemical energy → electrical energy → light and heat energy.
Work and Energy Work = Force × Distance   (W = F × d)
Diagram Description An energy transfer flow chart: Sun → plant (chemical energy in food) → person (kinetic energy) → bicycle moving down a road.
Worked Example A boy pushes a box with a force of 20 N through a distance of 3 m. How much work does he do?
W = F × d = 20 N × 3 m = 60 J
Exam Tip In energy-conversion questions, list the forms in order (input → output). Remember the sun is the ultimate source of most energy on Earth.

🚀 Force and Motion

Definition

Force is a push or a pull that can change the shape, speed or direction of a body. It is measured in newtons (N) with a spring balance. Motion is a change in position of a body with time.

Key Concepts

  • Contact forces: friction, air resistance, tension. Non-contact: gravity, magnetic, electrostatic.
  • Friction opposes motion and produces heat; it can be reduced with lubricants.
  • Speed = distance ÷ time.
Speed Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Weight Weight = Mass × g   (g ≈ 10 m/s²)
Diagram Description A box on the ground with two arrows: one pushing right (applied force) and one pulling left (friction). The box moves when the applied force is greater than friction.
Worked Example A car travels 240 km in 3 hours. Calculate its average speed.
Speed = 240 ÷ 3 = 80 km/h
Exam Tip Mass is in kilograms and never changes; weight is a force in newtons and changes with gravity. A 50 kg student weighs about 500 N on Earth.

🫀 The Human Body

Definition

The human body is made of cells that group into tissues, then organs, then systems. The main systems are the skeletal, muscular, digestive, respiratory, circulatory, nervous, excretory and reproductive systems.

Key Concepts

  • Digestive system: breaks food into nutrients; parts include mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small and large intestines.
  • Respiratory system: nose → trachea → bronchi → lungs; exchanges oxygen and carbon dioxide.
  • Circulatory system: heart, blood and blood vessels carry materials around the body.
  • Skeletal system: 206 bones support the body, protect organs and make blood cells.
Levels of Organisation Cell → Tissue → Organ → System → Organism
Diagram Description A labelled drawing of the human torso showing the heart, lungs, stomach, liver, kidneys and intestines in their correct positions.
Worked Example Name the organ that pumps blood around the body and the system it belongs to.
The heart; it belongs to the circulatory system.
Exam Tip Learn the correct order of the digestive tract and respiratory passage — sequencing questions are very common in JSS exams.
