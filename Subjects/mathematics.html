🔢 Logarithms

Definition

A logarithm is the power to which a base must be raised to produce a given number. If bʸ = x, then logb(x) = y. The logarithm and exponent are inverse operations.

Definition logb(x) = y  means  by = x

Laws of Logarithms

  • Product rule: log(ab) = log a + log b
  • Quotient rule: log(a/b) = log a − log b
  • Power rule: log(aⁿ) = n log a
  • Change of base: logb(x) = log a(x) / log a(b)
  • Special: log 1 = 0; logb(b) = 1
Diagram Description A number line showing powers of 10: 10⁰ = 1, 10¹ = 10, 10² = 100, 10³ = 1000. Below, the corresponding logarithms: log₁₀(1) = 0, log₁₀(10) = 1, log₁₀(100) = 2, log₁₀(1000) = 3. This illustrates that logarithms convert multiplicative relationships into additive ones.
Worked Example Given that log₁₀ 2 = 0.3010, find log₁₀ 5.
log₁₀ 5 = log₁₀(10/2) = log₁₀ 10 − log₁₀ 2
= 1 − 0.3010 = 0.6990
JAMB / WAEC Tip Always convert a log to its exponential form before solving. log₁₀ 0.01 = log₁₀(10⁻²) = −2 — logarithms of numbers between 0 and 1 are negative. Given log 2 = 0.3010 and log 3 = 0.4771, you can find any log by breaking the number into factors of 2 and 3.

📈 Sequences

Definition

A sequence is a set of numbers arranged in a definite order, following a pattern. The numbers are called terms. An arithmetic progression (AP) has a constant difference between consecutive terms; a geometric progression (GP) has a constant ratio.

Arithmetic Progression (AP)

nth term & Sum Tₙ = a + (n − 1)d  |  Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n − 1)d]

where a = first term, d = common difference, n = number of terms.

Geometric Progression (GP)

nth term & Sum Tₙ = arn−1  |  Sₙ = a(1 − rn)/(1 − r)  (r ≠ 1)

where r = common ratio.

Diagram Description AP pattern: 3, 7, 11, 15, 19... arrows between terms labelled "+4" showing the constant difference. GP pattern: 3, 6, 12, 24... arrows labelled "×2" showing the constant ratio.
Worked Example Find the 10th term of the AP: 3, 7, 11, 15, ...
a = 3, d = 7 − 3 = 4, n = 10
T₁₀ = 3 + (10 − 1)(4) = 3 + 36 = 39
Worked Example Find the 7th term of the GP: 2, 6, 18, ...
a = 2, r = 6/2 = 3, n = 7
T₇ = 2 × 37−1 = 2 × 729 = 1458
JAMB / WAEC Tip To find the common difference in an AP, subtract any term from the next: d = T₂ − T₁. To find r in a GP, divide any term by the previous: r = T₂/T₁. The nth term formula is always your starting point in sequence questions.

📐 Trigonometry

Definition

Trigonometry is the study of relationships between the angles and sides of triangles. It is fundamental in calculating distances, heights and angles in science and engineering.

SOH CAH TOA sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

Standard Values

  • sin 0° = 0  |  sin 30° = ½  |  sin 45° = √2/2  |  sin 60° = √3/2  |  sin 90° = 1
  • cos 0° = 1  |  cos 30° = √3/2  |  cos 45° = √2/2  |  cos 60° = ½  |  cos 90° = 0
  • tan 0° = 0  |  tan 30° = 1/√3  |  tan 45° = 1  |  tan 60° = √3  |  tan 90° = undefined
Identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
Diagram Description A right-angled triangle with the right angle marked. The angle θ is at the bottom-left vertex. The side opposite θ is on the right, the adjacent side is along the bottom, and the hypotenuse is the slanted side (opposite the right angle). Label each side O, A, H to remember SOH CAH TOA.
Worked Example If tan θ = 1 and θ is an acute angle, find θ.
tan θ = 1 → θ = tan⁻¹(1) = 45°
JAMB / WAEC Tip Memorise the standard angles 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° — they appear frequently. sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is the most used identity. For area of a triangle when two sides and the included angle are known: Area = ½ab sin C.

∫ Calculus Introduction

Definition

Calculus has two main branches: differentiation (finding the rate of change / gradient of a curve) and integration (finding the area under a curve / reversing differentiation).

Differentiation

Power Rule d/dx (xn) = n xn−1

The derivative dy/dx gives the gradient (slope) of the curve y = f(x) at any point x.

Integration (anti-differentiation)

Integral Power Rule ∫ xn dx = xn+1 / (n+1) + C   (n ≠ −1)

C is the constant of integration.

Diagram Description A parabola y = x² drawn on a graph. At the point (2, 4), a tangent line is drawn; its slope (gradient) is 4 — this is dy/dx = 2x evaluated at x = 2. Beneath the curve, the area between x = 0 and x = 1 and the x-axis is shaded; this area is found by integration.
Worked Example If y = 4x² − 2x, find dy/dx when x = 2.
dy/dx = 8x − 2
At x = 2: dy/dx = 8(2) − 2 = 16 − 2 = 14
JAMB / WAEC Tip Differentiation: bring the power down and reduce the power by 1. Integration is the reverse: increase the power by 1 and divide by the new power. Always add + C for indefinite integrals. The gradient of a straight line y = mx + c is simply m (constant).
