📚 Social Studies as a Discipline

Definition

Social Studies is the study of people, their environment and how they live and relate with one another. It draws knowledge from history, geography, civics, economics and sociology.

Key Concepts

  • Scope: the family, culture, citizenship, social institutions and social problems.
  • Objectives: to produce responsible citizens who can live and work with others.
  • It helps us understand our society and solve social problems.
Key Idea Social Studies = People + Environment + Relationships
Diagram Description A diagram with "Social Studies" at the centre connected by arrows to family, culture, citizenship and social problems.
Worked Example State two reasons why we study Social Studies in school.
1) To become a responsible citizen. 2) To understand and appreciate our culture and environment.
Exam Tip Be able to define Social Studies and list its objectives — short questions often start here.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 The Family

Definition

The family is the smallest and most basic unit of society. It consists of parents and their children living together. It is the first place where a child learns values and behaviour (primary socialisation).

Key Concepts

  • Nuclear family: father, mother and their children only.
  • Extended family: includes grandparents, uncles, aunts and cousins.
  • Functions: reproduction, care, socialisation, provision of needs and security.
  • Types of marriage: monogamy (one wife/husband) and polygamy (more than one).
Functions of the Family Reproduction · Care & Protection · Socialisation · Economic support · Emotional support
Diagram Description Two family trees side by side — a nuclear family (parents and children) and an extended family showing grandparents, uncles, aunts and cousins.
Worked Example Mention two members of an extended family who are not part of a nuclear family.
Any two of: grandparents, uncles, aunts, cousins, in-laws.
Exam Tip Learn the differences between nuclear and extended families, and be able to state the functions of the family.

🎭 Culture and Society

Definition

Culture is the total way of life of a people — their language, dress, food, music, religion, beliefs, values and customs. Society is a group of people living together under organised rules.

Key Concepts

  • Material culture: physical things (clothes, tools, food).
  • Non-material culture: beliefs, values, norms and language.
  • Cultural differences: variations between the ways of life of different groups.
  • Culture shock: the confusion felt when meeting a very different culture.
Culture Includes Language · Food · Dress · Music · Religion · Values · Customs
Diagram Description A picture wheel divided into segments showing different aspects of Nigerian culture — kolanut presentation, traditional attire, local dishes, drums and traditional festivals.
Worked Example Give two examples of non-material culture.
Any two of: religion, beliefs, values, language, customs, norms.
Exam Tip Be able to give examples of material and non-material culture from your own community.

🇳🇬 Citizenship

Definition

A citizen is a person who is a legal member of a country and enjoys its rights and privileges while also performing duties. Citizenship can be acquired by birth, registration or naturalisation.

Key Concepts

  • Rights: right to life, education, fair hearing, freedom of movement and association.
  • Duties: obeying laws, paying taxes, protecting public property, voting.
  • Ways to lose citizenship: renouncing it, or through fraud during acquisition.
Ways to Acquire Citizenship By Birth · By Registration · By Naturalisation
Diagram Description A balance scale: on one pan "Rights" (education, life, movement) and on the other "Duties" (obey laws, pay taxes, vote), showing that they balance.
Worked Example State two duties of a good citizen.
Any two of: obey the law, pay taxes, protect public property, vote, defend the nation.
Exam Tip Distinguish clearly between rights and duties, and learn the three ways of acquiring citizenship.

⚠️ Social Issues

Definition

Social issues are problems that affect the well-being of many people in a society. Common examples include drug abuse, cultism, examination malpractice, corruption, child labour and human trafficking.

Key Concepts

  • Drug abuse: misuse of drugs without prescription; harms health and leads to crime.
  • Cultism: secret groups that use violence; banned in schools.
  • Human trafficking: illegal movement of people for forced labour or exploitation.
  • Solutions: education, strict laws, guidance and counselling, parental care.
Curbing Social Issues Education · Law enforcement · Counselling · Parental care · Community awareness
Diagram Description A poster-style diagram showing "Say No to Drug Abuse" with a crossed-out drug symbol and helpline numbers below.
Worked Example Suggest two ways of reducing drug abuse among students.
Any two of: public enlightenment/education, guidance and counselling, strict laws, better parental supervision, encouraging positive activities.
Exam Tip For each social problem, learn at least two causes and two possible solutions — WAEC/JSS questions often ask both.
