🗳️ Democracy

Definition

Democracy is a system of government in which supreme power is vested in the people, exercised directly or through freely elected representatives. It is often defined as "government of the people, by the people, and for the people."

Features of Democracy

  • Popular sovereignty (power belongs to the people)
  • Free and fair periodic elections
  • Rule of law and equality before the law
  • Protection of fundamental human rights
  • Political pluralism (multi-party system)
  • Separation of powers among the three arms of government
WAEC Tip Sovereignty resides with the citizens in a democracy. Free and fair elections allow citizens to choose leaders and hold them accountable. Types of democracy: direct and representative.

⚖️ Human Rights

Definition

Human rights are the basic entitlements that belong to every person by virtue of being human. They are universal, inalienable and indivisible. In Nigeria, they are guaranteed in Chapter IV of the 1999 Constitution.

Categories of Rights

  • Civil rights: right to life, dignity, personal liberty, fair hearing.
  • Political rights: right to vote, be voted for, join a political party.
  • Economic rights: right to work, own property.
  • Social rights: right to education, health, shelter.

Rights and Responsibilities

Rights come with corresponding duties. Enjoying your rights means respecting the rights of others. Fundamental rights can be limited by law to protect national security and public order.

WAEC Tip The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR), adopted by the UN in 1948, is the foundational international human rights document. Remember: rights are not absolute — they can be limited when they infringe on others' rights.

🏛️ Rule of Law

Definition

The rule of law is the principle that every person and institution is subject to and accountable under the law. No one is above the law, including government officials and the President.

Principles (by A.V. Dicey)

  • Supremacy of the law — the law is above everyone.
  • Equality before the law — all citizens are equal regardless of status.
  • Guarantee of individual rights by the ordinary courts.

Separation of Powers

Government is divided into three arms with distinct functions to prevent abuse of power:

  • Executive: implements laws (President, Governors, Ministers).
  • Legislature: makes laws (National Assembly: Senate + House of Reps).
  • Judiciary: interprets laws (courts, headed by the Chief Justice of Nigeria).
WAEC Tip Separation of powers ensures checks and balances among the three arms. The Nigerian National Assembly has 109 senators and 360 House of Representatives members. The Chief Justice of Nigeria heads the judiciary.

🙋 Citizenship

Definition

A citizen is a legal member of a country with full rights and responsibilities. In Nigeria, citizenship may be acquired by birth, registration, or naturalisation.

Duties of a Citizen

  • Obeying the constitution and laws of the land
  • Paying taxes honestly
  • Voting in elections
  • Defending the nation and performing national service
  • Participating in community development
  • Respecting the rights and dignity of others

Patriotism

Patriotism is love for and loyalty to one's country, shown through civic participation, honest tax payment, and readiness to defend the nation.

WAEC Tip Section 25 of the Nigerian Constitution defines citizenship by birth. Rights and duties go hand-in-hand — a good citizen contributes positively to society. Patriotism includes respecting national symbols like the flag and anthem.
