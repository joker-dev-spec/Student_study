📚 Vocabulary

Definition

Vocabulary refers to the words a person knows and can use. WAEC tests synonym (same meaning), antonym (opposite meaning) and idiom recognition. Build your vocabulary by reading widely.

Key Vocabulary Pairs

  • Abundant ↔ Scarce — Plentiful, copious
  • Benevolent ↔ Malevolent — Kind, charitable
  • Frugal ↔ Wasteful — Thrifty, economical
  • Meticulous ↔ Careless — Extremely careful about details
  • Obstinate ↔ Obedient — Stubborn, unyielding
  • Transparent ↔ Opaque — See-through, clear
WAEC Tip In comprehension passages, always read the question first, then scan the passage for keywords. For synonyms/antonyms, use the context of the sentence to eliminate wrong options.

✏️ Grammar

Key Rules

  • Subject-verb agreement: He doesn't have (not "don't").
  • Active to Passive: "The boy ate the mango" → "The mango was eaten by the boy."
  • Reported speech: Tense shifts back: "I am going" → "He said he was going."
  • Conditional: "If I were you..." (subjunctive "were" for all persons).
  • Punctuate properly: Questions end with ?, names in direct address use commas.

Commonly Tested Plurals

  • Crisis → Crises · Focus → Foci · Analysis → Analyses · Criterion → Criteria
  • Child → Children · Leaf → Leaves · Knife → Knives · Mouse → Mice
WAEC Tip When converting to passive voice, the object becomes the subject and the verb changes to "be + past participle." Always match the tense of "be" to the original verb tense.

🎭 Figures of Speech

Definitions

  • Simile: comparison using "like" or "as". E.g. "as busy as a bee."
  • Metaphor: direct comparison without "like/as". E.g. "He is a lion in battle."
  • Personification: giving human qualities to non-human things. E.g. "The wind whispered."
  • Hyperbole: exaggeration for effect. E.g. "I've told you a million times."
  • Irony: saying the opposite of what is meant.
  • Oxymoron: contradictory words together. E.g. "deafening silence."

Idioms vs Proverbs

  • Idiom: an expression whose meaning is not literal. E.g. "kick the bucket" = die.
  • Proverb: a short saying expressing a general truth. E.g. "Many hands make light work."
WAEC Tip A simile must contain "like" or "as." If it does not, it is likely a metaphor. Personification gives human action to an object or animal. Study examples — WAEC often tests these in comprehension.
