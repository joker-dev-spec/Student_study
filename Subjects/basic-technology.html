🦺 Safety in the Workshop

Definition

Workshop safety means following rules and using equipment correctly to prevent accidents and injuries. Every workshop has safety rules, a first-aid box and a fire extinguisher.

Key Concepts

  • Wear protective clothing: overalls, goggles, gloves and boots.
  • Keep floors dry and tools tidy; never run in the workshop.
  • Do not use a machine unless you have been trained and the teacher is present.
  • Switch off and unplug machines after use.
Safety Colours Red = fire equipment · Green = first aid · Blue = mandatory · Yellow = warning
Diagram Description A workshop layout showing the workbench, tool rack, fire extinguisher near the door, first-aid box on the wall and clearly marked walkways.
Worked Example A student's clothes catch fire at a Bunsen burner. State one immediate action.
Do not run. Wrap the person in a fire blanket (or roll them on the floor) to cut off the oxygen and smother the flames.
Exam Tip Remember the meaning of safety colours and the correct use of a fire extinguisher (aim at the base of the fire, not the flames).

🪵 Materials and Their Properties

Definition

Technology uses many materials. The common ones are wood, metals, plastics, ceramics and glass. Each is chosen for projects based on its properties.

Key Concepts

  • Wood — hardwoods (mahogany, iroko) and softwoods (pine); easy to work, can rot or burn.
  • Metals — ferrous (contain iron, e.g. steel) and non-ferrous (aluminium, copper); strong, conduct heat and electricity.
  • Plastics — thermoplastics soften on heating (e.g. PVC), thermosetting plastics do not (e.g. bakelite).
Properties to Compare Strength · Hardness · Conductivity · Flexibility · Durability · Cost
Diagram Description A chart grouping materials: natural (wood, clay, rubber) and synthetic (plastics, glass, steel), with a common use beside each.
Worked Example Why is copper used for electrical wires?
Copper is a good conductor of electricity, is ductile (can be drawn into wires) and does not rust easily.
Exam Tip Be ready to match a material to a use and give a reason based on its properties, e.g. glass for windows because it is transparent.

📏 Technical Drawing

Definition

Technical drawing is the language of engineers. It uses precise lines, symbols and instruments to show the shape and size of objects before they are made.

Key Concepts

  • Drawing instruments: T-square, set squares (45° and 60°), compass, dividers, protractor, ruler, pencils (H and HB).
  • Line types: continuous thick (outline), continuous thin (dimension), dashed (hidden detail), chain (centre line).
  • Views: orthographic projection shows front, plan and side views.
Standard Sheet Sizes A4 (210 × 297 mm) · A3 (297 × 420 mm) · A2 (420 × 594 mm)
Diagram Description A simple wooden block drawn in three orthographic views: the front elevation (rectangle), the plan (top view) and the end elevation (side view), each properly aligned.
Worked Example Which drawing instrument is used to draw a circle of exact radius?
A pair of compasses (set to the required radius).
Exam Tip Learn the standard line types and their uses. Neatness, correct line weights and clear labelling earn marks.

🔧 Simple Machines

Definition

A machine is a device that makes work easier by changing the size, direction or speed of a force. Simple machines include the lever, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw and wheel and axle.

Key Concepts

  • Lever: a rigid bar turning about a pivot (fulcrum). Classes 1, 2 and 3 depend on the positions of fulcrum, load and effort.
  • Pulley: a wheel with a grooved rim; fixed pulleys change direction, movable pulleys reduce effort.
  • Inclined plane: a slope that reduces the effort needed to raise a load.
Mechanical Advantage MA = Load ÷ Effort
Diagram Description A class-1 lever (like a crowbar) with the fulcrum between the load and the effort, showing the effort arm and load arm distances.
Worked Example A load of 600 N is lifted with an effort of 200 N. Calculate the mechanical advantage.
MA = Load ÷ Effort = 600 ÷ 200 = 3
Exam Tip For levers, identify the class by the middle item: Fulcrum in the middle = Class 1; Load in the middle = Class 2; Effort in the middle = Class 3.

🔌 Electricity

Definition

Electricity is the flow of electric charge (electrons) through a conductor. A complete circuit is needed for current to flow. Current is measured in amperes (A) with an ammeter.

Key Concepts

  • Conductors allow current (copper, aluminium); insulators do not (rubber, plastic, dry wood).
  • Series circuit: one path; if one bulb fails, all go off.
  • Parallel circuit: separate paths; one bulb can fail while others keep working.
  • Circuit symbols: cell, battery, switch, bulb, resistor, ammeter, voltmeter.
Ohm's Law V = I × R   (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
Diagram Description A simple circuit: a battery, a switch and a bulb connected with wires in one loop. Closing the switch completes the circuit and lights the bulb.
Worked Example A current of 2 A flows through a resistance of 5 Ω. Find the voltage.
V = I × R = 2 × 5 = 10 V
Exam Tip Learn to draw and recognise circuit symbols. Remember: series = same current, parallel = same voltage across each branch.
